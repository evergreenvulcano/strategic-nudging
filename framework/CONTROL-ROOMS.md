# Control Rooms

A **control room** is a domain of mastery that becomes necessary when an empire grows large enough to expose it.

Control rooms are not deficiencies. They are not lessons. They are not corrections.  
They are **rooms that a growing empire eventually enters** — because it is large enough to need them.

---

## The Metaphor

The empire grows. With growth comes surface. Each new surface brings a new type of reality into contact with the work. That reality demands a form of governance — a control room — where the operator can read signals, adjust, and maintain sovereignty over that surface.

The key insight: **the control room does not exist because the builder failed. It exists because the empire succeeded enough to need it.**

---

## The Universal Control Room Set

These are the control rooms that appear in virtually every empire of sufficient scale:

---

### Revenue Control Room
*"How do we make the traffic commercially readable?"*

Covers: advertising markets, monetization logic, pricing strategy, conversion architecture, revenue operations.

Activates when: the empire has users, content, or attention but has not yet built a mechanism that converts presence into sustainable income.

---

### Trust Control Room
*"How do we protect what is now worth something?"*

Covers: cybersecurity posture, user data handling, authentication integrity, vulnerability surface, abuse prevention.

Activates when: the empire begins to hold value that others might target — user data, financial transactions, platform reputation.

---

### Scale Control Room
*"How do we ensure growth doesn't become a cost surprise?"*

Covers: cloud infrastructure, hosting architecture, operational cost modeling, capacity planning, performance under load.

Activates when: traffic, data, or transaction volume begins to outpace the assumptions built into the original infrastructure.

---

### Survival Control Room
*"How do we decide which parts of the empire prove themselves first?"*

Covers: business model clarity, prioritization logic, unit economics, resource allocation, strategic focus.

Activates when: the empire has more surfaces than can be simultaneously proven — and needs a selection logic.

---

### Reality Control Room
*"How do we make the world itself show us the next move?"*

Covers: analytics, user behavior data, funnel visibility, A/B testing, feedback loops, product instrumentation.

Activates when: decisions are being made based on intuition in a context that now generates enough signal to inform them empirically.

---

### Accountability Control Room
*"How do we govern what the empire is responsible for?"*

Covers: legal structure, terms of service, privacy compliance, liability surfaces, intellectual property.

Activates when: the empire begins to operate at a scale where third parties — users, regulators, partners — have legitimate claims.

---

### Behavior Control Room
*"How do we shape how users move through the empire?"*

Covers: support operations, moderation policy, community governance, user experience design, onboarding architecture.

Activates when: users exist in sufficient numbers that their collective behavior becomes a surface that requires active management.

---

### Legibility Control Room
*"How do we ensure the empire can be understood by others?"*

Covers: documentation, onboarding for contributors, API contracts, partner integration surfaces, public communication of what the work is.

Activates when: the empire is sophisticated enough that its value is invisible to outsiders without translation.

---

## How to Use This Taxonomy

When producing an expansion map for a case:

1. **Assess which control rooms are already active.** The empire may already be operating them, even informally.
2. **Identify which control rooms are now being exposed by growth.** These are the priority rooms.
3. **Name each room with a question** — not a demand. The question invites the operator in, rather than pointing at a gap.
4. **Sequence by exposure.** The control rooms that the empire's current scale most directly exposes come first.

The expansion map is not a to-do list. It is a **territory map** — it shows which rooms exist, which are lit, and which are dark. The operator decides when to enter each room.
